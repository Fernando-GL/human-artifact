The Spread of the Printing Press in Europe

The Beginning of European Printing

Around the 1450s, Johannes Gutenberg developed a system of movable-type printing in Mainz, Germany. This technology made it possible to reproduce books more efficiently than copying them by hand.

Source: University of St Andrews

Printing Spreads Across Europe

During the following decades, printing activity began spreading beyond its early centers. Printing presses appeared in more European cities and the technology started reaching new regions.

Source: University of St Andrews

A Growing Network of Printing Centers

By around 1500, printing had spread across much of Europe. Important cities such as Venice, Paris, Basel, and other commercial centers became important locations for printing and publishing.

Source: University of St Andrews

Printing Becomes Established

During the sixteenth century, printing became an established industry in many European cities. Books, religious texts, academic works, and other printed materials could circulate across larger areas.

Source: University of St Andrews

European Printing Networks Expand

By the beginning of the seventeenth century, printing activity was connected to a large network of cities. Major commercial and intellectual centers played an important role in producing and distributing printed information.

Source: University of St Andrews

A Widespread Technology

By 1650, printing activity had appeared in more than 1,300 European locations. Printing had become an important technology for distributing information, knowledge, and ideas.

Source: University of St Andrews
